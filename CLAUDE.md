## Brand Rules (Gulf Sovereign Intelligence)
- Research, not investment advice. No introductions or guaranteed access.
- Evidence first: investor-specific amount separate from headline value; explicit status; cite source, retrieval date, as-of date.
- Never call data complete or decision-grade before QA sign-off. Unreviewed month is never "zero deals".
- GSI v4.9 schema is locked. Records are append-only and versioned.
- Public publishing, exports and subscriptions need the founder's approval. "Publishing" means actively selling or promoting GSI. Live testing of the structure (repo, Supabase test database) is allowed when the founder asks for it, and the data stays unreviewed and not decision-grade.
- British spelling. Avoid hype words.

## Strategic focus
Evidence-backed Gulf sovereign investment reporting for institutional decision-makers (investment committees, strategy and corporate development teams, institutional investors, advisers, government investment agencies). Not an investor-matching marketplace. GSI competes on analytical rigour and decision usefulness, not database size.

Value proposition: know where Gulf sovereign capital is moving, why it matters, and what decisions the evidence supports.

Every report answers five questions: what happened; what is verified; what is changing; why it matters; what decision it informs.

## Strategic priorities
1. Complete the historical evidence base, with consistent classification and source traceability.
2. Produce decision-level fund profiles: PIF, Mubadala, ADIA, ADQ, QIA, KIA, OIA and relevant affiliated entities.
3. Develop cross-fund comparative intelligence: sector priorities, geographic shifts, investment partners, capital deployment, strategic divergence.
4. Publish concise executive briefs backed by deeper evidence dossiers (publishing needs the founder's approval).
5. Validate willingness to pay with institutional readers before expanding the product or committing to a subscription model.

## Product direction (proposed, not yet validated)
Two products on one research base. Neither is committed until priority 5 (willingness to pay) is tested.
- Decision Intelligence: what a transaction reveals about Gulf capital strategy (investment committees, institutional investors, strategists).
- Capital Intelligence: what it tells us about eligibility, financing routes and relationships (CFOs, corporate development, fund managers, advisers). Decision support only: no brokering, no introductions, no placement.
- Not a global investor marketplace. Gulf focus only. Global SWF and the Sovereign Wealth Fund Institute already sell sovereign profiles and mandates, so GSI's edge is evidence quality and vehicle-level analysis.
- Name the investing vehicle, not just the fund (for example the Mubadala platform or subsidiary), plus the instrument, co-investors and sector wherever the evidence shows them.
- Separate publicly disclosed mandates from mandates inferred from past deals. Past activity does not prove a fund is seeking a similar deal now.
- A match is "research further" until mandate, ticket size, geography and current deployment are verified.
- The v4.9 schema stays locked. Any new field (for example mandate type or eligibility) needs a founder ruling first.

## Report structure
Every substantive report uses these sections, in order:
1. Executive judgment: what decision-makers need to understand.
2. Verified developments: dates, entities, amounts, primary-source references.
3. Strategic interpretation: what the evidence suggests, with alternative explanations.
4. Implications: who may benefit, face risks or need to reconsider assumptions.
5. Forward indicators: specific developments worth monitoring.
6. Evidence audit: confidence, contradictions, unresolved questions.

## Editorial rules
- Distinguish announced, committed, completed and deployed capital.
- Never treat total transaction value as the fund's own investment without evidence.
- Separate confirmed facts, analytical inferences and hypotheses.
- Give competing explanations for significant strategic conclusions.
- Treat undisclosed information as unknown, not zero.
- Record corrections and keep traceability to original sources.
- The data schema has no "deployed" field yet; until the founder rules, record deployment evidence in the existing capital-deployed and status fields.
